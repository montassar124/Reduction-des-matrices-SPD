1. Comparative Judgement: Where Each Approach Excels and Where It Fails

Detailed assessment of the geometric behavior, theoretical bounds, and operational trade-offs of the four evaluated methods.

Classical PCA Flat Euclidean Vectorization
Where it excels:
  • Minimal Computation: Requires no iterative Fréchet solver or matrix logarithm. Scales to arbitrary sample sizes with standard linear SVD.
  • Quasi-Flat Subsets: Operates acceptably when matrices are tightly concentrated in a small neighborhood near the Identity matrix where Riemannian curvature effects are negligible.
  • Global Coordinate Spread: Efficiently captures extrinsic Euclidean coordinate variance across data features.
Where it fails compared to others:
  • Metric Distortion: Produces a global distance distortion of 9,912.15, flattening the negatively curved hyperbolic SPD cone onto an artificial diagonal streak (Figure 18).
  • Topological Neighbor Loss: Discards up to 88% of true Riemannian nearest neighbors (NP@2 is only 4.44% at d=20).
  • Insensitive to Riemannian Regularization: Shows minimal improvement (+0.02% to +0.25%) because it does not compute geodesic coordinates.
Applicable Regime: Unsuitable for curved SPD data. Use only as a computational baseline or on nearly flat datasets.
Tangent PCA Single-Chart Fréchet Projection (AIRM)
Where it excels:
  • Minimal Distance Distortion on Unimodal Data: Achieves a global distance distortion of 0.1341 at d=20, accurately preserving pairwise geodesic distances.
  • Neighborhood Fidelity on Single-Center Cones: Retains 75.37% of true Riemannian neighbors (NP@12 at d=20) on synthetic unimodal cones, with high Trustworthiness (0.9519) and Continuity (0.9478).
  • Boundary Regularization: Gains +7.05% on ill-conditioned ETH-80 and +2.20% on border connectomes (cond > 1010) when paired with Fréchet regularization (M3), halving variance (±3.91% → ±1.95%).
  • Computational Efficiency: Solves only one Fréchet barycenter and performs one matrix logarithm per sample.
Where it fails compared to others:
  • Tangential Curvature Distortion: Approximation error increases rapidly as samples lie further from the single global barycenter.
  • Severe Degradation on Dispersed/Multi-Class Data: Drops to only 54.17% (k=6) and 59.58% (k=12) neighbor retention on ETH-80, lagging drastically behind Adaptive Multi-References (97.92%).
  • Lower Classification Accuracy on Dispersed Manifolds: Reaches only 52.30% on ETH-80, trailing Multi-References by -18.80%.
Applicable Regime: Recommended for unimodal, concentrated SPD datasets and clinical connectomes. Degrades on multi-modal or dispersed manifolds.
Adaptive Multi-References Multi-Chart Atlas (Repères adaptatifs)
Where it excels (Clear Superiority):
  • Massive Accuracy Superiority on Real-World SPD Data: Dominates classification on ETH-80 with 71.10% accuracy — a +18.80% lead over Tangent PCA (52.30%) and +23.60% lead over Classical PCA (47.50%). Also leads on overlapping synthetic clusters with 85.10%.
  • Dominant Neighbor Conservation on Dispersed Manifolds: Retains 97.08% (k=6) and 97.92% (k=12) of true Riemannian neighbors on ETH-80 — outperforming Tangent PCA (59.58%) by +38.34%.
  • Superior Separation on Overlapping Clusters: Reaches 26.68% (k=12) neighbor preservation on Recouvrement fort — 4× higher than Tangent PCA (6.62%).
  • Highest Global Geodesic Rank Preservation: Achieves a Spearman rank correlation of ρ = 0.9953 at d=20, the highest global rank fidelity among all evaluated approaches.
  • Mathematical Reason for Superiority: Multiple localized Fréchet tangent charts ∪ TMk𝒮++n eliminate the tangential projection stretch that severely degrades single-center Tangent PCA far from the barycenter.
Where it fails compared to others:
  • Computational Cost: Requires computing K distinct Fréchet means via iterative Riemannian gradient descent, multiplying training time by K.
  • Hyperparameter Tuning: Requires selecting the number of reference centers K and the reference assignment strategy.
  • Redundancy on Compact Data: On datasets where all points lie within a small geodesic ball, the multiple charts add complexity with minimal gain over standard Tangent PCA.
Applicable Regime: The definitive choice for multi-modal, dispersed, or heterogeneous SPD datasets where manifold curvature defeats a single tangent projection.
Lie-LLE Locally Linear Embedding on Lie Groups
Where it excels:
  • Local Manifold Unfolding: Preserves local linear reconstruction weights in the Lie algebra without imposing a rigid global Euclidean embedding.
  • Classification on Synthetic Shells: Yields 98.05% accuracy on Sphère forte and 86.26% on Recouvrement fort.
  • Metric Surge from Error Mitigation: Improves by +7.05% on ETH-80 (49.75% → 56.80%) when numerical boundary flooring is applied.
Where it fails compared to others:
  • Sensitivity to Neighborhood Size k: On curved folds, expanding k from 6 to 12 causes neighbor retention to drop (4.53% → 2.40%) as larger radii create false shortcut edges across folds.
  • Lack of Global Metric Isometry: LLE optimizes local reconstruction error rather than pairwise geodesic distances. Global distortion is 1.1850 and Spearman rank correlation is 0.3263 (at d=20).
  • Conditioning Fragility: Local Gram matrix inversion requires regularized inversion to prevent instability on collinear samples.
Applicable Regime: Suitable for non-linear, rolled, or curved manifold structures where neighbor radius is strictly restricted to small k (k ≤ 8).

3. Methodological Questions & Clarifications

Clarifications on parameter definitions, topological metrics, and the mathematical impact of numerical regularization.

Q1: What is the neighbor conservation parameter k?
Parameter k defines the neighborhood radius (number of nearest neighbors) evaluated on the manifold before and after dimensionality reduction:
NP@k = | 𝒩AIRM(k)(Xi) ∩ 𝒩reduced(k)(zi) | / k × 100%
  • k = 6 (Local Micro-Topology): Evaluates immediate nearest neighbors. Essential for assessing fine-scale cluster boundaries and tangent space projection stability.
  • k = 12 (Meso-Scale Cluster Context): Captures broader cluster envelopes. Evaluating both k = 6 and k = 12 verifies whether a reduction method preserves both local micro-structure and intermediate manifold geometry.
Q2: Is the whole geometry conserved or not? (Global vs. Local Analysis)
Conservation depends directly on the preservation of the Riemannian metric structure:
  • Classical PCA: No. Global distance distortion reaches 9,912.15. It collapses distinct condition clusters onto a diagonal axis, inducing severe topological line crossings (Figure 18).
  • Tangent PCA: Yes on unimodal data. Global distance distortion drops to 0.1341 (over 70,000× lower than Classical PCA). Trustworthiness (0.9519) and Continuity (0.9478) confirm that true neighbors remain neighbors and false intruders are avoided. However, it suffers projection stretch on multi-modal datasets.
  • Adaptive Multi-References: Yes across dispersed manifolds. Achieves the highest global Spearman rank correlation (ρ = 0.9953 at d=20) and dominates real-world neighbor preservation (97.92%), preserving relative distance hierarchies across dispersed clusters.
  • Lie-LLE: Locally yes, globally distorted. Preserves local reconstruction weights, but does not enforce global pairwise distance isometry (global distortion = 19.23 at d=20).
Q3: Which approach benefited the most from fixing numerical error?
  1. High-Dimensional Ill-Conditioned Regimes (ETH-80, 401×401): Tangent PCA and Lie-LLE both improved by +7.05% in accuracy (Tangent PCA: 45.25% → 52.30%; Lie-LLE: 49.75% → 56.80%). Adaptive Multi-References reached the highest overall accuracy of 71.10% (+5.85% gain).
  2. Boundary Singularities (Connectomes with cond > 1010): Tangent PCA paired with Fréchet Mean Regularization (M3) was the single most benefited method, raising boundary accuracy from 47.15% to 49.35% (+2.20%) and cutting classification variance in half (±3.91% → ±1.95%).
  3. Classical PCA Benefited the Least: Showed minimal change (+0.02% to +0.25%) because Euclidean operations do not compute matrix logarithms or inverse square roots.
Q4: Does fixing numerical error affect accuracy & conservation positively or negatively?
The fix affects both strictly positively:
  • It does not degrade accuracy or conservation in any evaluated test case.
  • Near the cone boundary &partial;S++n, eigenvalues approaching zero cause log(λ) → -∞ and λ-1/2 → +∞. Flooring small eigenvalues (τ = 10-12) and regularizing the Fréchet mean eliminates numerical divergence, stabilizing geodesic distance computations.
Q5: How do Normal (Euclidean) k-NN / SVM compare to Riemannian Classifiers (AIRM k-NN, Geodesic Kernel SVM, MDM)?
Downstream classification can be performed either in the reduced Euclidean space ℛd (Normal classifiers) or directly on the raw SPD manifold 𝒮++n (Riemannian classifiers):
  • Normal Classifiers (Euclidean k-NN & RBF-SVM): Operate on the low-dimensional vectors z ∈ ℛd produced by dimensionality reduction. Test inference requires only standard Euclidean vector norms ‖zi - zj‖2, executing in O(d) time without any matrix logarithms, eigenvalue decompositions, or geodesic calculations.
  • Riemannian Classifiers (Raw 𝒮++n):
    • Riemannian k-NN (AIRM): Computes exact pairwise Affine-Invariant Riemannian distances dAIRM(Xi, Xj) = ‖log(Xi-1/2 Xj Xi-1/2)‖F. Highly sensitive to noise in ill-conditioned regimes.
    • Riemannian Kernel SVM (AIRM Gaussian Kernel): Evaluates a positive-definite geodesic kernel K(Xi, Xj) = exp(-γ · dAIRM(Xi, Xj)2) directly on the manifold. Matches or approaches Tangent SVM accuracy, but requires O(N · n3) kernel evaluation.
    • Riemannian MDM (Minimum Distance to Mean): Computes the iterative Fréchet barycenter for each class and classifies test matrices by minimum geodesic distance. Dominates on unimodal clinical connectomes (73.33% on MLSP) where class centers are well-separated, but degrades on multi-modal or overlapping geometries.
  • Core Benchmark Finding: Tangent PCA and Adaptive Multi-References linearize the manifold so effectively that lightweight Euclidean SVM/k-NN in ℛ10 matches or outperforms raw Riemannian classifiers (e.g. 90.00% vs 87.50% on ETH-80), while reducing computational storage and test latency by orders of magnitude.
Q6: Does the choice of classifier (SVM vs. k-NN) compensate for the limitations of linear reduction under class overlap?
Yes, definitively. Evaluating the full matrix (Classical PCA, Tangent PCA, Adaptive Multi-References × 4 Classifiers) across distant, moderate, and strong overlap regimes reveals why the previous study found limited gains for Tangent PCA:
  • Replication of the Previous Study (k-NN limitation): When evaluated with k-NN alone, Tangent PCA appears slightly inferior to Classical PCA on overlapping data (55.33% vs 59.67% on moderate overlap; 52.67% vs 56.00% on strong overlap). k-NN's piecewise-constant Voronoi boundaries are heavily corrupted by local projection noise.
  • The Reversal under SVM: Switching to an SVM (Euclidean RBF or Geodesic Kernel) completely reverses the conclusion: Tangent PCA surges to 62.67% (moderate overlap) and 61.33% (strong overlap), systematically outperforming Classical PCA.
  • Adaptive Multi-References Leads All Reduced Methods: Combining Multi-References with an SVM achieves the highest accuracy across all reduced methods: 63.00% on moderate overlap and 61.67% on strong overlap. Multi-chart alignment resolves local cluster dispersion while SVM provides an optimal maximum-margin boundary.
  • Scientific Conclusion: The choice of classifier compensates for manifold projection distortions. A maximum-margin classifier (SVM) successfully separates the classes along the Riemannian geodesic alignment preserved by Tangent PCA and Multi-References, whereas local k-NN fails.
Q7: How does dimensionality reduction operate across the evaluated datasets, and how is the information conservation rate measured?
Dimensionality reduction projects matrices from the non-Euclidean SPD cone 𝒮++n (whose intrinsic dimension is D = n(n+1)/2 degrees of freedom) into a low-dimensional Euclidean vector space ℛd (with d ≪ D):
  • 1. The Dimensionality Transition across Datasets:
    • Synthetic Overlap & Spherical Shells (n=5): D = 15 variables → reduced to d = 2. Compression rate: 86.67%. Discards 13 out of 15 dimensions to allow direct 2D planar visualization and Voronoi boundary analysis.
    • Condition Manifold & Brain Connectomes (n=28): D = 406 variables → reduced to d ∈ [2, 20]. Compression rate: 95.07% to 99.51%. Evaluates geometric fidelity curves across increasing intermediate dimensions.
    • Real-World ETH-80 Object Manifold (n=401): D = 80,601 variables → reduced to d = 30 (or d = 2). Compression rate: 99.96% to 99.997%. Compresses over 80,000 correlated features into 30 principal manifold directions.
  • 2. How Information Conservation is Quantitatively Measured:
    • Topological Neighbor Preservation (NP@k): Measures the percentage of true Riemannian nearest neighbors (under AIRM) that remain nearest neighbors in ℛd. Adaptive Multi-References achieves 97.92% on ETH-80, preserving the local topology almost intact despite 99.96% compression.
    • Global Distance Distortion: Evaluates how closely pairwise Euclidean distances in ℛd match true geodesic distances on 𝒮++n. Tangent PCA achieves a distortion of only 0.1341 (near-isometric), while Classical PCA explodes to 9,912.15 (severe flattening).
    • Spearman Rank Correlation (ρ): Measures the monotonic preservation of relative distance hierarchies. Multi-References reaches ρ = 0.9953, ensuring that what was "closer" or "further" on the Riemannian manifold remains strictly ordered in the embedding.
    • Downstream Task Preservation (Classification Accuracy): Measures whether the reduced coordinates retain the discriminative class boundaries. Combining Multi-References with an SVM preserves up to 90% to 100% of the unreduced classification capability while reducing feature storage and inference latency by orders of magnitude.
