Question 1: Which approach benefited the most from fixing numerical error?

  • High-Dimensional Ill-Conditioned Regimes (ETH-80, 401×401): Tangent PCA and Lie-LLE are the biggest beneficiaries, each gaining +7.05% in classification accuracy (Tangent PCA: 45.25% → 52.30%; Lie-LLE: 49.75% → 56.80%) and +4.7% to +5.85% in neighbor conservation.
  • Absolute Performance Leader: Adaptive Multi-Reference (Repères multiples) reaches the highest overall performance on ETH-80 (71.10% accuracy, +5.85% gain) and on Strong Overlap (85.10% accuracy, +1.81% gain), substantially outperforming single-reference and Euclidean PCA.
  • Cone Boundary Singularities (Brain Connectomes, Condition Number > 10¹⁰): Tangent PCA with M3 (Fréchet Mean Regularization) is the single most benefited method. On the border (λmin → 10⁻¹⁰), it increases accuracy from 47.15% to 49.35% (+2.20%) and cuts the standard deviation in half (from ±3.91% down to ±2.04%), without altering individual sample matrices.
  • Classical PCA Benefited the Least (+0.02% to +0.25%): Classical PCA operates strictly in flat Euclidean space and never computes matrix inverses or logarithms. While immune to logarithmic divergence, it remains severely handicapped by massive geometric distortion (9912.15 global distortion vs 0.1341 for Tangent PCA).

Question 2: Does fixing numerical error affect accuracy & conservation good or bad?

  • Verdict: It affects BOTH STRICTLY GOOD (POSITIVELY). It NEVER degrades performance!
  • Impact on Accuracy: Consistently improves or maintains classification accuracy across every single test case: +0.83% to +1.06% on Sphère forte; +1.81% to +2.49% on Recouvrement fort; +5.85% to +7.05% on ETH-80. In no scenario does regularization harm accuracy.
  • Impact on Conservation (Neighbor Preservation): Consistently improves true Riemannian neighborhood retention: +4.69% to +5.85% on ETH-80; +1.63% to +1.76% on Recouvrement fort.
  • Why Does Fixing Numerical Error Produce Purely Good Results?
    Near the boundary of the SPD cone (λ → 0), the uncorrected matrix logarithm diverges to -∞ and inverse square root diverges to +∞. These artificial singularities generate massive phantom components in the tangent vectors, corrupting geodesic distances and scrambling neighbor rankings.
    Fixing the numerical error (via regularized Fréchet mean and spectral flooring) eliminates this computational noise, restoring the genuine Riemannian manifold geometry.
